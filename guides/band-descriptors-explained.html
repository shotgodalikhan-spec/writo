Task 2 band descriptors explained simply

The official band descriptors are written for examiners. Here is what the main levels mean in practice, so you know what to change to move up.

Band 5: the message is there, but limited

The essay answers the question only partly, ideas are not developed, and there may be no clear conclusion. Organisation is visible but linking is repetitive. Vocabulary is limited and errors sometimes make reading hard.

Band 6: relevant and clear, with problems

All parts of the task are addressed, but some ideas are not fully developed or the conclusion is unclear. Paragraphs are used, linking is sometimes faulty or mechanical. There is enough vocabulary for the task, with some errors that don't block understanding. A mix of simple and complex sentences, with some mistakes.

Band 7: well developed and controlled

A clear position throughout, with main ideas extended and supported. Logical organisation and a range of linking used flexibly. Some less common vocabulary used with awareness of style, and only occasional errors. Frequent error-free sentences and good control of grammar.

Band 8: precise and natural

A well-developed response with relevant, extended and supported ideas. Cohesion is managed well and paragraphs are logical. Wide vocabulary used fluently and precisely, with rare slips. Most sentences are error-free.

How the overall writing score works

Each criterion gets its own band. Task 2 counts for more of the writing score than Task 1, which is why it is worth practising carefully. See your estimated band for each criterion.
