How to check your Writing Task 2 essay

Before you ask anyone for a score, run your essay through this checklist. It follows the four criteria examiners use, so you can fix the problems that cost the most points first.

1. Task Response: did you answer the question?

  • Underline every part of the task. Questions like "Discuss both views and give your opinion" have three parts, and missing one caps your score.
  • Say your position clearly in the introduction and keep it the same until the conclusion.
  • Each body paragraph needs one main idea, explained and supported with a specific example.
  • Write at least 250 words. Shorter essays are penalised.

2. Coherence and Cohesion: is it easy to follow?

  • Use clear paragraphs: introduction, two or three body paragraphs, conclusion.
  • Start each body paragraph with a topic sentence.
  • Use linking words where they help, not in every sentence. "Firstly, Moreover, Furthermore, In addition" in a row sounds mechanical.
  • Check that "this", "it" and "they" always point to something clear.

3. Lexical Resource: is your vocabulary precise?

  • Replace vague words (things, good, bad, a lot) with precise ones.
  • Avoid repeating the same key word; use natural alternatives.
  • Check spelling and word forms (economy, economic, economical).
  • Don't force rare words you are unsure of. Accuracy beats showing off.

4. Grammatical Range and Accuracy: are your sentences correct?

  • Mix simple and complex sentences: relative clauses, conditionals, passive voice.
  • Check subject–verb agreement: "the advantages outweigh", not "outweighs".
  • Check articles (a, an, the) and plurals.
  • Aim for many sentences without any error. Error-free sentences matter for higher bands.

5. Then get a second opinion

Once you have fixed what you can, check your essay with Writo to see an estimated band range for each criterion and your mistakes highlighted in the text.
